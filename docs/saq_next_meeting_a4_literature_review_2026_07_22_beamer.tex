\begin{frame}{1. The Short Answer}
\begin{block}{Original question}
Can one fixed-width word work better if two scalar coordinates receive arbitrary numbers of reconstruction levels?
\end{block}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We finally completed the missing GIST/CIFAR base-data comparison.
  \item Arbitrary scalar cardinalities produced essentially no held-out gain.
  \item A same-capacity two-dimensional codebook produced a clear gain.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{alertblock}{Decision}
The missing flexibility is the \textbf{shape of the joint codebook}, not the number of scalar levels. The original formulation stops before query testing.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{2. First Avoid One Old Misunderstanding}
The original Attempt 4 was \textbf{not} progressive pruning.
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.47\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Original Attempt 4}
Read one full word once. Change scalar level counts. Use one mixed-radix lookup.
\end{block}
\column{0.47\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Later prefix successor}
Read a short prefix, then more bits. Add coarse filtering and accurate refinement.
\end{block}
\end{columns}
\begin{alertblock}{Keep the conclusions separate}
The prefix successor was separately closed as a direct composition of known mechanisms. Today's result concerns the original single-lookup idea.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{3. Original Intuition: Use a Four-Bit Word More Fully}
Two scalar coordinates share one four-bit word: 16 possible addresses.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccl}
\toprule
Model & Allocation & Joint states\\
\midrule
ordinary & 2 bits $+$ 2 bits & $4\times4=16$\\
ordinary & 1 bit $+$ 3 bits & $2\times8=16$\\
Attempt 4 & arbitrary $K_1,K_2$ & $K_1K_2\leq16$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{block}{Example}
$(K_1,K_2)=(3,5)$ uses 15 addresses. Three levels on one coordinate and five on the other might fit unequal coordinate difficulty better than every power-of-two allocation.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{4. Core Method: Fit, Pack, Look Up}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fit scalar quantizers for every permitted level count.
  \item Choose $K_1,K_2$ with the smallest summed scalar error.
  \item Pack scalar labels $z_1,z_2$ into one label:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{verbatim}
u = z1 + K1 * z2
T[u] = distance(query pair, reconstructed pair u)
\end{verbatim}
The scan still reads one word and performs one lookup.
\begin{alertblock}{The key restriction}
All centers form a rectangular product $\{a_i\}\times\{b_j\}$. Changing $K_1,K_2$ changes the number of rows and columns; it cannot move one joint center independently.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{5. Running Example: What $(3,5)$ Can and Cannot Prove}
\begin{center}
$x_1\in\{-1,0,1\}$ \hspace{10mm} $x_2\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$
\end{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X c c}
\toprule Model & States & Error\\
\midrule arbitrary scalar product $(3,5)$ & 15 & $0$\\
ordinary power-of-two product $(4,4)$ & 16 & positive\\
unrestricted 16-center 2D codebook & 16 & $0$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\begin{block}{Claim ceiling}
The example proves that power-of-two scalar allocation can waste choices. It does not prove natural-data, Recall, or speed gains. The unrestricted 2D codebook also fits exactly.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{6. Four Arms Answer Four Different Questions}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{c >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\textwidth} X}
\toprule & Plain-language meaning & Question\\
\midrule
\texttt{D} & power-of-two scalar grid & What does the ordinary rectangular restriction achieve?\\
\texttt{A} & arbitrary scalar cardinalities & Does changing only row/column counts help?\\
\texttt{P} & independently trained ordinary PQ & Does a standard strong implementation dominate?\\
\texttt{V} & same-capacity 2D vector codebook & How much is available if joint centers move freely?\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\normalsize
\begin{block}{Why both P and V?}
P is the deployed-family control. V is the stronger opportunity test: it tells us whether the data has useful 2D structure that A fails to express.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{7. What Was Actually Measured}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\bfseries}p{0.29\textwidth} X}
datasets & GIST and CIFAR\\
fitting / held-out & 8,192 / 8,192 base residuals per dataset\\

No benchmark query or ground-truth file was read. This is offline base-data evidence, not Recall or throughput evidence.
\end{alertblock}

\begin{frame}{8. Main Result: A Did Not Generalize}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Dataset/rate & D error removed by A & D-to-V gap closed & pair-proxy gain & groups helped\\
\midrule
GIST B4  & $0\%$       & $0\%$        & $0\%$        & $0/64$\\
GIST B8  & $0.0928\%$  & $0.5396\%$   & $0.1237\%$   & $7/64$\\
CIFAR B4 & $0\%$       & $0\%$        & $0\%$        & $0/64$\\
CIFAR B8 & $-0.0455\%$ & $-0.4186\%$  & $-0.1252\%$  & $6/64$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\normalsize\par
\medskip
At B4, A chose $(4,4)$ everywhere. At B8, it chose non-power-of-two allocations in 14 GIST and 8 CIFAR groups, so the larger family was genuinely exercised.
\begin{alertblock}{Registered decision}
Those choices still produced no material held-out benefit. Every materiality hypothesis for A failed after multiplicity correction.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{9. The Important Positive Observation: Two Dimensions Help}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Reduction from ordinary scalar grid to 2D VQ & B4 & B8\\
\midrule
GIST  & $11.4\%$ & $17.2\%$\\
CIFAR & $8.8\%$  & $10.9\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All four registered tests for the existence of this opportunity passed.
\begin{block}{Interpretation}
The result is not ``there is nothing to improve.'' Natural residual pairs contain useful 2D structure, but changing only the two scalar alphabet sizes does not capture it.
\end{block}
\begin{alertblock}{Geometric intuition}
A rectangular grid can add rows or columns. A 2D codebook can place every center where the data actually needs it.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{10. Lower Reconstruction Error Is Still Not Recall}
\begin{center}
\large lower reconstruction error\\[1mm]
$\Downarrow$ \textit{sometimes, not automatically}\\[1mm]
lower query-to-database distance error\\[1mm]
$\Downarrow$ \textit{sometimes, not automatically}\\[1mm]
higher Recall at matched throughput
\end{center}
\normalsize
We measured the first and a base-pair proxy for the second. A failed both.
\begin{alertblock}{Why query evaluation stopped}
Continuing to benchmark Recall or QPS would spend effort on a formulation that already failed its cheaper discriminating test.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{11. Closest Work I: PQ and OPQ}
\paper{Product Quantization (PQ)} splits a vector into blocks, learns one vector codebook per block, and accumulates query distance from small tables.
\medskip
\paper{Optimized Product Quantization (OPQ)} jointly learns a rotation and the block codebooks:
\begin{center}
\plain{rotate} $\rightarrow$ \plain{split into blocks} $\rightarrow$ \plain{nearest centers} $\rightarrow$ \plain{store labels}
\end{center}
\begin{block}{Boundary}
OPQ can improve which dimensions are grouped and how variance is distributed. ``Learn a rotation, then quantize'' is therefore not a new mechanism. OPQ is a required baseline for any future 2D model.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{12. Closest Work II: AQ and CQ}
\paper{Additive Quantization (AQ)} reconstructs a vector as a sum:
\[
  x \approx c_1[i_1]+c_2[i_2]+\cdots+c_m[i_m].
\]
This is expressive, but encoding and distance evaluation are harder.
\medskip
\paper{Composite Quantization (CQ)} constrains interactions between dictionaries so query distances can still be evaluated from lookup tables.
\begin{alertblock}{Boundary}
``Build one center from several learned pieces'' is already covered. A new model needs a stricter database property---for example one fixed label, one lookup, or materially cheaper table construction.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{13. Closest Work III: Optimize the Consumed Error}
Standard PQ usually minimizes reconstruction error for individual vectors.
\medskip
\paper{Pairwise Quantization} learns a linear transform so the following quantizer better preserves pairwise squared distances or scalar products:
\begin{center}
\plain{base-pair statistics} $\rightarrow$ \plain{learned transform} $\rightarrow$ \plain{ordinary quantizer}
\end{center}
\begin{block}{Why it matters}
Our consumer is a distance estimator. Pairwise-oriented training is therefore an important baseline objective.
\end{block}
\begin{alertblock}{Claim boundary}
Changing from reconstruction loss to a known pairwise loss is not itself a contribution; Recall and systems performance still have to move.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{14. Closest Work IV: Systems Costs}
\paper{Quicker ADC} studies irregular subcode granularity, packed layouts, split lookup tables, and SIMD scanning. Nonstandard packing must beat an optimized scan.
\medskip
\paper{SegPQ} compresses PQ codebooks and supports query processing over the compressed representation. It reports up to $4.7\times$ codebook compression with about $3.3\%$ added query-processing overhead.
\begin{alertblock}{What reviewers will ask}
A smaller codebook or different label layout is insufficient. Measure table construction, cache behavior, decoding, Recall, and throughput together.
\end{alertblock}
\scriptsize ADC means query-to-code distance lookup; SIMD means one processor instruction handles several values in parallel.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{15. The Original Idea Is Already Crowded}
\small
\begin{itemize}
  \item \paper{Muresan--Effros}: optimal 1D quantization for integer level counts.
  \item \paper{Brandt}: learned scalar quantizers, integer bit allocation, packed words, tables, and ANN scan.
  \item \paper{FSQ}: products of small scalar level sets, including non-power-of-two factors.
  \item \paper{BAPQ}: unequal integer bit allocation among PQ subspaces.
  \item \paper{Mixed-radix indexing}: standard finite-alphabet addressing.
  \item \paper{Q-Palette / FibQuant}: broader dense-rate coding territory.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
\begin{alertblock}{New empirical closure}
Even where arbitrary level counts were selected, they did not recover the observed 2D opportunity.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{16. A Better Modeling Question}
\begin{block}{New question}
Can a compact structured 2D codebook recover most of full 2D VQ quality while preserving a fixed label and cheap lookup?
\end{block}
One example is a shared-shape affine model:
\begin{verbatim}
center[g,k] = mean[g] + transform[g] * shared_shape[k]
\end{verbatim}
\begin{itemize}
  \item one reusable non-rectangular 2D shape;
  \item a small scale/rotate/shear transform for each group;
  \item one fixed-width database label $k$; and
  \item one query table and one lookup per group.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{17. Why That Is Not Yet a Contribution}
At B8, a rough FP32 shared-affine representation is about 3.5 KiB; independent 2D centers use 128 KiB.
\medskip
But one global 128-KiB model is already small.
\begin{alertblock}{The systems question}
Compression matters only in a setting with many local codebooks, many probed cells, or frequent updates, where table construction, cache, memory, or update cost is measured to be material.
\end{alertblock}
Without a systems effect, ordinary 2D PQ/VQ is the simpler answer. SegPQ also prevents us from claiming codebook compression alone.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{18. Cheapest Discriminating Next Check}
Before building another ANN path:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item fit the shared-shape affine model on the existing fitting panels;
  \item evaluate it on the existing held-out panels against D and V;
  \item measure how much of the D-to-V gap it recovers;
  \item count model bytes and table-building arithmetic; and
  \item stop if it needs nearly unrestricted per-group centers or recovers little of V's gain.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{block}{Escalation rule}
Only a strong base-only result would justify a later native table-build and scan comparison. Freeze the model before touching benchmark queries.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{19. Final Decision}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\bfseries}p{0.36\textwidth} X}
arbitrary scalar cardinalities & \texttt{CLOSED: NO\_GO\_BASE\_ONLY}\\
same-capacity 2D opportunity & real on both datasets and rates\\
Recall / QPS & not measured\\
prefix successor & separately closed as direct composition\\
structured 2D successor & a new question, not yet established\\
\end{tabularx}
\begin{alertblock}{Meeting takeaway}
The original $(K_1,K_2)$ idea is not the right model for natural residuals. Evidence points to joint 2D geometry, but a future direction must also move a real database-systems cost frontier.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}
